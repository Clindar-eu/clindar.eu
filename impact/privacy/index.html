What leaves your browser, and how to check

This page is written for whoever has to sign off on running a tool against study metadata. It states what the SDTMIG v4.0 Impact Scanner reads, what it transmits, and how to confirm both from your own devtools without trusting a word of it.

Applies to clindar.eu/scanner. Last reviewed 31 August 2026.

The short version

The Define-XML you drop in is parsed by JavaScript running in your browser tab. It is not uploaded, not proxied, not queued, and not logged. It is never sent anywhere, because the page it runs on is served with a policy that leaves it nowhere to send it.

One thing can leave, and only if you type it: an email address, after your score is on screen, if you join our rules-catalogue mailing list. It goes to /.netlify/functions/subscribe on this domain. Nothing about your scan goes with it.

Your report does not travel either — in either direction. It is written by the same JavaScript that read your file and downloads out of the tab as HTML, Markdown or JSON. We never receive it, which is also why we cannot email it to you and do not offer to.

What a Define-XML contains, and what the scanner reads

A Define-XML is study metadata, not study data: the datasets in a submission, the variables in each, their types, lengths and roles, the codelists and their terms, value-level metadata, comments, computation methods and origins. It normally carries the study identifier and protocol name, and it can carry free text that someone wrote by hand — comments and method descriptions are the usual places.

It does not contain subject data. No USUBJIDs, no visits, no results, no adverse events. Whether your organisation nonetheless treats a Define as confidential is your call, and a reasonable one: it describes the shape of a trial.

The scanner reads the dataset and variable definitions, codelists, value-level metadata and the standards declarations. It reads them to decide which SDTMIG v4.0 changes apply to your study. Everything it reads stays in the tab's memory and is gone when you close it — nothing is written to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, or a cookie. Reload the page and the scan is gone with it.

What the scanner page is permitted to contact

Every response under /scanner/ carries this Content-Security-Policy header:

default-src 'self'; base-uri 'self'; object-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'; form-action 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; font-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; connect-src 'self'

connect-src 'self' is the directive that matters. It means the page's JavaScript can open a network connection to exactly one origin — this one — and to no other. Not to an analytics service, not to a font host, not to a CDN, not to an error reporter, not to us by any other route. A browser enforces this; it is not a setting the page can opt out of.

Why that says 'self' and not 'none'. connect-src 'none' is the stricter directive — it permits no network connection at all, and it is what this page would carry if the scanner did nothing but score your file. It says 'self' for one reason: the mailing-list signup that appears under your score needs a request to somewhere, and that somewhere is a path on this domain. We would rather tell you that than quietly ship the weaker policy and keep describing the stronger one.

What 'self' does not do is widen the claim about your data. It permits requests to this origin and to no other; the only request the scanner ever makes under it is the POST you trigger by joining the list; and that request carries the address you typed and nothing else. Your Define-XML is not in it. It is never read into anything that could put it there, and it does not cross the network under either policy — which is the part you can check for yourself below, rather than take from us.

The same page also loads no third-party script of any kind. The scanner's own JavaScript, its stylesheet and the email capture widget all come from this domain. Our marketing pages do use Plausible, a cookieless analytics service — the scanner does not, and neither does this page.

Verify it yourself, in about two minutes

  1. Open clindar.eu/scanner and open devtools before you do anything else (F12, or Cmd-Option-I on a Mac).
  2. Read the policy. In the Network tab, reload, click the first document request, and look at the response headers. You should see content-security-policy with connect-src 'self' in it. It is also repeated in a <meta> tag in the page source, which the browser enforces as well.
  3. Watch the scan. Leave the Network tab open, tick "Preserve log", and drop in a Define-XML. Nothing new appears. No XHR, no fetch, no beacon, no image request with your data hidden in the query string. The score arrives with no network activity at all, because the work happened in the tab.
  4. Try to break it. If you want to be certain the policy is doing the work rather than the page merely behaving, open the Console and run fetch('https://example.com'). The browser refuses it and says why. That refusal is what stands between your Define-XML and the internet.
  5. Watch the one request that does happen. If you join the mailing list, a single POST to /.netlify/functions/subscribe appears. Click it and read the request payload: it is {"email":"…"} and nothing else — no file name, no study identifier, no score, no rule ids.
  6. Download the report and watch nothing happen. Click "Download HTML report". The file appears with no network request behind it, because the page built it from what was already in memory. That is the same fact from the other side: a document we never receive is a document we cannot email you.

If any of that does not match what you see, we would rather hear about it than not: info@clindar.eu. The configuration is what would be wrong, and we would fix it.

The email address, if you give us one

What is collected
The email address you type, and the hour it arrived. Nothing else is recorded: no IP address, no browser, no referrer, no scan result.
Why
To send you the SDTMIG v4.0 change checklist, and to tell you when the rules catalogue changes. It is a mailing list: every subscriber receives the same emails, because your scan is not something we hold and could tailor them to.
What is never sent to you
Your scan report. It is produced in your browser and downloaded from it, and no copy reaches us — so no email we send can contain it, whatever you subscribe to.
Legal basis
Consent, GDPR Article 6(1)(a). You gave it by typing the address and asking to join the list. Withdraw it at any time with the unsubscribe link on every email, or by writing to us.
Who else sees it
Our email service provider, as a processor, which stores the address and sends the list. It is never sold, never shared for anyone else's marketing, and never joined to anything you scanned — we do not have that to join it to.
How long
Until you unsubscribe, or after two years without a single opened email, whichever comes first.
Your rights
Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, under GDPR Articles 15–21. Write to info@clindar.eu and we will action it. You may also complain to your national supervisory authority.
Rate limiting
The endpoint counts recent submissions per caller for an hour, keyed by a salted hash of the IP address held in memory only. The address itself is never written down and the salt is discarded when the process restarts.

There are no cookies on the scanner. Not analytics cookies, not preference cookies, not "essential" cookies. There is no consent banner because there is nothing to consent to.

If a browser is still the wrong place

Some organisations will not run study metadata through a web page whatever the headers say, and that position is defensible. The same engine — the same rules catalogue, the same scoring — runs as a command-line tool inside your own network, with no browser and no network access at all. Write to us and we will get you a build.
